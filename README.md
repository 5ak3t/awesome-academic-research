## Research Databases & Repositories

The foundation. Where you actually find literature.

- [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/) — AI-powered academic search across 200M+ papers with TLDR summaries, citation graphs, and personalized feeds. Free. Built by the Allen Institute for AI.
- [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/) — Fully open catalog of 250M+ scholarly works, authors, institutions, and concepts. The best free replacement for Microsoft Academic. Has a public API.
- [PubMed / PubMed Central](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) — Authoritative biomedical and life science index from NIH. 35M+ citations. MeSH vocabulary for precision searching.
- [Web of Science](https://webofknowledge.com) — Curated citation index across disciplines. Institutional access usually required.
- [Scopus](https://www.elsevier.com/solutions/scopus) — Broad cross-disciplinary coverage with AI-powered trend identification.
- [arXiv.org](https://arxiv.org/) — Open access preprints for physics, math, CS, economics, and more. 2M+ papers.
- [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/) — Journals, books, and protocols.
- [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/) — Electrical engineering, CS, and electronics.
- [ACM Digital Library](https://dl.acm.org/) — Computer science and information technology.
- [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/) — Aggregates 200M+ open access research papers from repositories worldwide. Free.
- [Dimensions AI](https://www.dimensions.ai/) — Links publications to grants, patents, and clinical trials. 100M+ papers.

## Preprints & Open Access

- [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/) — The gold standard preprint server for CS, physics, math, economics, and quantitative biology.
- [bioRxiv / medRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/) — Preprints for biology and medicine. 400K+ papers; ~80% go on to peer-reviewed publication within 250 days. 48–72 hour posting turnaround.
- [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/) — Preprints for social sciences, law, economics, and humanities.
- [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/) — Chemistry preprints from the Royal Society of Chemistry.
- [OSF Preprints](https://osf.io/preprints/) — Multidisciplinary preprints from the Center for Open Science.
- [Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/) — Browser extension that finds legal free versions of paywalled papers automatically. Integrates with Google Scholar and Web of Science.
- [Open Access Button](https://openaccessbutton.org/) — Request or find legal free copies of paywalled research.

## AI Research Assistants

> Full-pipeline tools covering multiple research stages. Match the tool to your workflow stage, not the hype.

- [Fynman](https://fynman.com/) — **Privacy-first desktop workspace** for literature review and manuscript drafting. Runs entirely on your machine — PDFs never leave your computer. Analyzes your whole library simultaneously. Supports Zotero/Mendeley import, offline LLMs, PRISMA-compliant systematic reviews, and AI writing assistance. $49/year. Best for: researchers with sensitive data, GDPR/HIPAA constraints, or large libraries.
- [Elicit](https://elicit.org/) — Automated literature review across 138M papers (Semantic Scholar + PubMed + OpenAlex). Structured data extraction, AI-generated reports, systematic review workflows, and research alerts. 80%+ time savings reported. Best for: systematic reviews and structured evidence synthesis.
- [Consensus](https://consensus.app/) — Search engine surfacing direct citation-backed answers from 200M+ peer-reviewed papers. Consensus Meter shows level of scientific agreement. Best for: quick evidence checks, yes/no research questions.
- [SciSpace](https://typeset.io/) — AI Copilot for reading: highlighted answers within papers, thematic comparison tables, and custom literature review templates. Best for: reading-heavy workflows and multi-paper synthesis.
- [Paperguide](https://paperguide.ai/) — Semantic search, automated literature review, deep research reports, reference management, and AI writing in one platform. Free tier; paid from $12/month. Best for: students and researchers wanting an all-in-one tool.
- [Perplexity AI](https://perplexity.ai/) — Real-time cited web answers. Not academic-database-first, but solid for fast background research on current developments.
- [ResearchPal](https://researchpal.co/) — AI writing assistant with Zotero integration and literature review generation.

## Literature Discovery & Mapping

> Finding papers and understanding how they relate to each other.

- [Research Rabbit](https://researchrabbitapp.com/) — Visual network of papers connected by citation and co-authorship. Import from Zotero. Free. Adapts to your reading patterns. Access to 270M+ papers.
- [Connected Papers](https://www.connectedpapers.com/) — Similarity graph around a seed paper, revealing foundational and derivative work. Essential for rapidly scoping a field.
- [Litmaps](https://www.litmaps.com/) — Dynamic citation maps with real-time alerts for new matching publications. Tracks how a field evolves over time.
- [Scite](https://scite.ai/) — Smart Citations: classifies 1.5B+ citations as supporting, contrasting, or mentioning. Tells you not just *who* cited a paper, but *how* — critical for assessing whether a finding holds up.
- [Inciteful](https://inciteful.xyz/) — Two free tools: Paper Discovery (similar papers) and Literature Connector (path between two papers).
- [Open Knowledge Maps](https://openknowledgemaps.org/) — Concept map grouping papers by theme from a keyword or paper. Good for rapid field orientation.
- [Lateral](https://www.lateral.io/) — Upload papers; find semantically similar ones from your library or the web.
- [Keenious](https://keenious.com/) — Paste text or upload a draft; finds related literature based on your content. Good for gap-checking while writing.
- [Papers With Code](https://paperswithcode.com/) — Links ML/AI papers to code and datasets. Essential for reproducible machine learning research.

## Systematic Review & Evidence Synthesis

> The most methodologically demanding research task. These tools exist specifically for this workflow.

- [Rayyan](https://www.rayyan.ai/) — Global standard for title/abstract screening. Free. AI "Predictor" learns your inclusion/exclusion decisions and ranks remaining papers by predicted relevance, cutting screening time by up to 50%. Blind collaboration mode. PRISMA flow diagram support. Mobile app. Used by 350K+ researchers at 15,000 organizations.
- [Covidence](https://www.covidence.org/) — Full systematic review management: screening, full-text review, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment. Endorsed by Cochrane. Free for Cochrane authors; $240/review otherwise; institutional plans available. Best for: rigorous multi-reviewer systematic reviews with full audit trails.
- [ASReview Lab](https://asreview.nl/) — Open-source active learning that uses your decisions to prioritize relevant papers for screening. Fully transparent algorithms — fully reproducible and defensible. Best for: researchers who need methodological accountability.
- [DistillerSR](https://www.evidencepartners.com/products/distillersr-systematic-review-software/) — Enterprise-grade platform covering the full review workflow: screening, extraction, risk-of-bias, and reporting. Built for large teams and complex multi-phase reviews.
- [EPPI-Reviewer](https://eppi.ioe.ac.uk/) — Web-based tool for systematic reviews and evidence maps; particularly strong in social science and education. Supports PICO extraction and thematic synthesis.
- [Robot Reviewer](https://www.robotreviewer.net/) — Free AI tool that semi-automates data extraction from RCTs: PICO elements, study design, and risk-of-bias flags. From the Cochrane Collaboration.
- [Elicit](https://elicit.org/) — Also covers extraction: structured tables from hundreds of papers simultaneously. Integrates well with Rayyan/Covidence output.

## Reading & PDF Analysis

> Tools specifically for understanding individual papers faster.

- [SciSpace Copilot](https://typeset.io/) — Ask questions about any paper; get cited answers with text highlights. Browser extension and upload mode.
- [Explainpaper](https://www.explainpaper.com/) — Highlight confusing passages; get plain-language explanations. Good for reading outside your immediate subfield.
- [AskYourPDF](https://askyourpdf.com/) — Upload and chat with single or multiple PDFs.
- [R Discovery](https://discovery.researcher.life/) — Personalized paper recommendations with summaries, audio streaming, and 30-language translation. Free.
- [Jenni.ai](https://jenni.ai/) — In-editor PDF chat and autocomplete. Chat with your sources as you write.

## Citation & Reference Management

> Get this right from day one. Retroactively organizing references is painful.

- [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/) — The best free, open-source reference manager. Browser plugin captures metadata automatically. Group libraries, Word/Docs/Overleaf plugins, device sync. The default choice for most researchers.
- [Mendeley](https://www.mendeley.com/) — Reference manager with PDF annotation and Elsevier journal integration. Free desktop app.
- [Paperpile](https://paperpile.com/) — Clean, fast, cloud-native reference manager. Deep Google Docs integration. $3/month for students.
- [JabRef](https://www.jabref.org/) — Open-source BibTeX manager for LaTeX users. Essential if you write in LaTeX.
- [Citavi](https://www.citavi.com/) — Reference management plus knowledge organization and structured writing support. Popular in German-speaking academia.
- [Citation Gecko](https://citationgecko.azurewebsites.net/) — Find related papers through citation network seeding. Free.
- [VOSviewer](https://www.vosviewer.com/) — Bibliometric network visualization: co-authorship, co-citation, keyword co-occurrence. Also listed under Data Visualization.

## Academic Writing & Editing

> Writing tools built specifically for scholarly work.

- [Writefull](https://writefull.com/) — Language feedback trained on published academic papers. Catches non-native phrasing, weak hedging, and terminology inconsistency. Integrates with Word and Overleaf. Highly recommended for non-native English writers.
- [Paperpal](https://paperpal.com/) — AI writing assistant backed by 23+ years of STM publishing data. Grammar, paraphrasing, plagiarism, consistency, and journal submission readiness checks. Word, Google Docs, and Overleaf integration. Free tier available.
- [Thesify](https://www.thesify.ai/) — Designed specifically for PhD thesis and dissertation writing. Gives chapter-level structural feedback and helps align literature reviews to your research gap. Not a grammar checker — a thinking tool for doctoral writers.
- [Jenni.ai](https://jenni.ai/) — In-editor AI autocomplete, PDF citation insertion, and writing assistance while drafting. Useful for getting past blank-page paralysis.
- [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/) — Collaborative LaTeX editor in the browser. Standard for math, physics, engineering, and CS papers. Free tier is generous; paid plans from $21/month.
- [Typeset / SciSpace](https://typeset.io/) — Automated journal formatting. Switches your manuscript between journal templates in seconds.
- [Articlo](https://getarticlo.com/sample-package) — Prepares an existing Word manuscript for a supported target journal and returns an editable DOCX, change summary, and author checklist. Human editorial and reference-validity review remain essential.
- [Predatory Journals Watchlist](https://predatoryjournals.com/) — Check before submitting to an unfamiliar journal.

## Qualitative Data Analysis

- [NVivo](https://lumivero.com/products/nvivo/) — Industry-standard QDA for coding interviews, surveys, and multimedia. AI-assisted initial coding and theme clustering. Strong audit trail for methodological transparency.
- [ATLAS.ti](https://atlasti.com/) — QDA with AI-proposed codes, network visualization of concepts, and team collaboration. Shows how AI suggestions were generated — methodologically defensible.
- [MAXQDA](https://www.maxqda.com/) — Mixed-methods QDA; strong for combining qualitative and quantitative data in one project. Good visualization tools.
- [Delve](https://delvetool.com/) — Simpler, browser-based thematic analysis tool. Good for researchers who find NVivo overwhelming.

## Quantitative & Statistical Analysis

- [R + RStudio / Posit](https://posit.co/) — The open-source standard for statistical computing in academia. Unmatched ecosystem for statistics, data science, and visualization (ggplot2, tidyverse, Shiny).
- [MATLAB](https://www.mathworks.com/products/matlab.html) — Dominant in engineering, signal processing, and neuroscience. Deep domain-specific toolboxes.
- [SPSS](https://www.ibm.com/spss) — GUI-driven statistical analysis widely used in social sciences and health research.
- [Stata](https://www.stata.com/) — Strong in economics and epidemiology. Excellent for panel data and causal inference.
- [JASP](https://jasp-stats.org/) — Free, open-source GUI for Bayesian and frequentist statistics. Designed for transparent, reproducible analysis.
- [jamovi](https://www.jamovi.org/) — Free, open-source stats tool built on R with a spreadsheet-like interface. Good for researchers transitioning from SPSS.
- [Bibliometrix (R package)](http://www.bibliometrix.org/) — Bibliometric and science mapping analysis: publication trends, citation networks, keyword co-occurrence.

## Data Visualization

- [VOSviewer](https://www.vosviewer.com/) — Bibliometric network maps: co-authorship, co-citation, and keyword co-occurrence. Essential for mapping a field's intellectual structure.
- [CiteSpace](https://citespace.podia.com/) — Visualizes emerging trends and structural shifts in scientific literature over time.
- [RAWGraphs](https://www.rawgraphs.io/) — Open-source, browser-based tool for custom data visualizations. No coding required.
- [Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/) — Publication-ready charts and maps quickly. Free tier is solid. Used by major newspapers.
- [Flourish](https://flourish.studio/) — Interactive charts and data stories for research communication beyond journal figures.

## Research Communication & Publishing

- [OSF (Open Science Framework)](https://osf.io/) — Pre-register studies, share data and materials, collaborate openly. Connects to GitHub, Zotero, and institutional repositories. Free.
- [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/) — CERN-hosted open repository for datasets, software, presentations, and papers. Free, citable DOIs.
- [Figshare](https://figshare.com/) — Share and cite research outputs: figures, datasets, posters, presentations.
- [ORCID](https://orcid.org/) — Persistent researcher identifier. Non-negotiable — connect your publications, grants, and affiliations across every platform. Free.
- [PREreview](https://prereview.org/) — Open preprint review platform. Submit or request structured peer review. 2,000+ reviewers in 170+ countries.
- [Altmetric](https://www.altmetric.com/) — Track online attention your papers receive: news, policy documents, social media, Wikipedia.
- [Publons / Web of Science Reviewer Recognition](https://publons.com/) — Record and verify your peer review history and editorial contributions.

## Research Integrity & Ethics

- [iThenticate](https://www.ithenticate.com/) — Plagiarism detection used by publishers and institutions. Check manuscripts before submission.
- [Retraction Watch Database](https://retractionwatch.com/) — Searchable database of retracted papers. Check papers you're citing — especially in fast-moving fields.
- [Scite](https://scite.ai/) — See whether papers you rely on have been subsequently supported or contradicted in the literature.
- [COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics)](https://publicationethics.org/) — Guidelines and case studies on authorship, peer review, data integrity, and publishing ethics.
- [Predatory Journals Watchlist](https://predatoryjournals.com/) — Before submitting to any unfamiliar journal, check here.
